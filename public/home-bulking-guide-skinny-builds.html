Bottom line up front
  1. You have to eat more than you burn, consistently. Skinny people usually overestimate how much they eat. Track your food for a few weeks so you know.
  2. Aim for a modest surplus: roughly 300–500 extra calories a day for a beginner, targeting about 0.25–0.5% of bodyweight gained per week. Very skinny beginners can often go a little faster (around 0.5–1 lb a week).
  3. Hit about 1.6 g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day (about 0.7 g per lb). Benefits flatten out above that, though some people benefit up to about 2.2 g/kg.
  4. Nut-free throughout. Food suggestions avoid peanuts and tree nuts. Check labels on powders and packaged foods for cross-contact.
  5. Drink some of your calories. Shakes, milk and calorie-dense nut-free foods (sunflower seed butter, oats, olive oil) are the standard fix for a small appetite. So are more frequent meals and not overdoing cardio.
  6. Bodyweight training builds muscle if sets are hard. Taken close to failure, push-ups produced muscle growth comparable to bench press in studies. The key is progressing to harder variations over time. A pull-up bar and a pair of adjustable dumbbells cover the gaps, especially for legs.
  7. Weigh yourself daily and compare weekly averages. If the average isn't rising, add about 100–150 calories a day and recheck in two weeks.
Part 01

How much to eat

The principle is simple: eat more calories than your body burns, consistently, and you'll gain weight, however fast your metabolism feels.1 The catch is that skinny people tend to overestimate how much they eat, so tracking what you actually eat matters more than going by feel.12

How big a surplus? The evidence points to "modest":

  • In one study of trained lifters, bigger calorie surpluses mostly added fat rather than extra muscle or strength. The write-up's rule of thumb is that 200–400 extra calories a day is plenty for trained lifters.3
  • Beginners can use more. One review-based guide suggests 300–500 extra calories a day in your first year of training, dropping to 200–300 for intermediates.4 A common starting point for skinny "hardgainers" is 300–500 calories above your daily burn.5
  • On rate of gain, one guide recommends about 0.25–0.5% of bodyweight per week for beginners.6 MacroFactor's research review found gains up to about 0.5% per week led to large lean-mass increases with little to no fat gain on average.7
  • Skinny beginners are the exception that can go faster. Bony to Beastly, a coaching site for thin men, says many skinny beginners gain more than a pound a week without visible fat. In a study it cites, 1 lb/week bulkers gained 2.6 lb muscle and 0.7 lb fat over 4 weeks, versus 5.3 lb muscle and 4 lb fat for 2 lb/week bulkers.8

The landmark Morton meta-analysis (49 studies, 1,863 people) found that protein beyond about 1.62 g per kg per day added no further muscle from training.10 A 2022 meta-analysis of 74 trials found more protein gave small extra gains in lean mass, with the effect showing up in people under 65 who ate 1.6 g/kg or more while training.13

Practical points:

  • Total daily protein matters more than precise timing, but spreading it across meals helps. One study found evenly distributed protein produced 25% higher muscle protein synthesis over 24 hours than a skewed pattern.14
  • 20–40 g of casein (in milk, cottage cheese or casein powder) before bed stimulates protein synthesis overnight.12
  • For hardgainers, more protein beyond the target isn't the answer. Protein is filling, so one guide suggests not exceeding about 2.2 g/kg and getting the extra calories from carbs and fats instead.15

For scale: 140 lb ≈ 102 g protein a day at 1.6 g/kg; 160 lb ≈ 117 g.11

Part 03

How to actually eat more

The hard part for most skinny people isn't knowing the number. It's eating that much without feeling stuffed. These are the tactics hardgainer coaches keep coming back to.

Biggest lever

Drink calories

Liquid calories are much less filling than the same calories in solid food,16 which is why weight gainers come as shakes. A whole apple is far more filling than juice made from the same apple.15 One coach suggests keeping liquids to about 10–15% of daily intake, favoring milk and juice over soda.17

Easy win

Eat more often

Eating 3,000 calories over three sittings is much harder than spreading the same amount over six meals.17 A self-described hardgainer recommends at least four meals a day, ideally 5–6, and says liquids count as meals.18

Choose dense foods

Hardgainer guides usually lean on peanut butter and nuts.19 Nut-free swaps with similar calorie density: sunflower seed butter, pumpkin and sunflower seeds, avocado. Other easy adds: olive oil on everything, whole milk, oats, rice, cheese and dried fruit.

Don't burn it off

Hardgainer guides warn against too much cardio16 and suggest cutting non-lifting calorie burn while bulking.18 Some skinny people simply move a lot without noticing (fidgeting, pacing, walking everywhere).17

Increase gradually

Raise your intake week by week instead of jumping straight to a huge number.18 Your stomach adapts. Expect to eat more as you get heavier, since a bigger body burns more at rest.1

Measure, don't guess

A food scale and a tracking app for the first few weeks show what two tablespoons of seed butter or olive oil actually looks like, and whether you're really eating what you think.2

Peanut and tree nut allergy note. Everything in this guide is written to be nut-free, but cross-contact is the real risk. Protein powders, mass gainers, granola, protein bars, oats and seed butters are often made on shared equipment with peanuts or tree nuts. Choose products labeled as made in a peanut- and tree-nut-free facility, read every label for "may contain" warnings, and recheck when a brand changes its recipe. Ask your allergist before adding seeds or coconut if you aren't sure they're safe for you.
Part 04

Can you really build muscle at home?

Yes, with two conditions: the sets have to be hard, and the difficulty has to increase over time.

  • An 8-week study found push-ups at a load similar to 40% of a person's bench-press max, done to failure, were comparably effective for muscle growth and strength as bench press.20 A 2018 study found progressively harder push-up variations produced strength gains comparable to bench press over 4 weeks.21
  • Lighter loads build similar muscle to heavy ones when sets are taken close to failure. A Journal of Physiology study found load didn't dictate muscle growth in trained lifters training to failure.22
  • You don't have to hit absolute failure. A 2022 meta-analysis found training to failure wasn't better than stopping 1–2 reps short.23 Constant failure adds fatigue that can hurt later workouts.24
  • The American College of Sports Medicine's 2026 resistance-training guidance supports bodyweight exercise as effective resistance training.24

The weak spot is legs and pulling muscles. External weight makes small increases easier to measure and is especially useful for leg training and for muscles that are hard to load with bodyweight alone.24 That's why a minimal home setup is worth it.

Minimal equipment, in order of value

1st buy

Doorway pull-up bar

Pulling is the hardest pattern to train without equipment. A bar gives you chin-ups, pull-ups, hanging knee raises and, set low, rows.

2nd buy

Adjustable dumbbells

Lets legs progress past bodyweight (goblet squats, split squats, Romanian deadlifts), plus rows, presses and curls.

Cheap add

Resistance bands / loaded backpack

Band training shows results consistent with weights when you keep progressing.23 A backpack filled with books adds load to push-ups and squats.

How to keep progressing without heavier weights

Progression can mean more reps and sets, harder leverage, single-limb versions, a bigger range of motion, or slower lowering. One guide suggests a "3-0-2" tempo: 3 seconds down, no pause, 2 seconds up.2323 Going from three sets of 10 push-ups to four sets of 12 over four weeks is progressive overload.23

Part 05

A starter home program

Three or four full-body sessions a week, alternating Day A and Day B (for example Mon A, Wed B, Fri A, then Mon B the next week). Do 3 sets per exercise, each ending about 1–3 reps short of failure, with 1.5–2 minutes rest. The structure follows the push / pull / knee / hip / core split used in a calisthenics hypertrophy plan.24 Pick the rung of the ladder above that puts you in 8–20 reps.

Day A

  1. Push-up variation, 3 × 8–20
  2. Chin-up or row variation, 3 × 6–15
  3. Reverse lunge or split squat, 3 × 10–15 per leg
  4. Hip bridge or RDL, 3 × 10–20
  5. Dead bug or hollow hold, 3 sets

Day B

  1. Pike push-up variation, 3 × 6–15
  2. Row variation (bar, table or dumbbell), 3 × 8–15
  3. Squat variation (goblet or Bulgarian), 3 × 10–15
  4. Single-leg hip bridge, 3 × 10–15 per leg
  5. Hanging knee raise, 3 × 8–15

Every week

  1. Log reps for every set.
  2. Try to beat last week by one rep or one harder variation.
  3. Add a 4th set to lagging areas after 4–6 weeks.
  4. Keep cardio light: walks, not long runs.
  5. Sleep 7–9 hours; growth happens during recovery.
Part 06

Track and adjust

Daily weight swings with food, water and salt, which confuses people.1 Weigh every morning under the same conditions and compare weekly averages. Daily weight can swing 1–2 kg, but the weekly average is meaningful.27

Your weekly average is…Do this
Not moving, or below targetAdd about 100–150 calories a day (an extra glass of milk or a spoonful of sunflower seed butter) and hold for two weeks. Guides suggest increments of 50–150 calories or 2.5–5% of intake.2728
On targetKeep calories the same. Expect to raise them over time as you get heavier.1
Well above target (e.g. 2+ lb/week after the first month)Trim about 100–150 calories a day.27

Reps are going up

The best sign the weight is muscle. Log every set.

Measurements

Monthly: waist, chest, upper arm, thigh. A growing arm with a steady waist is a good bulk.

Photos

Same lighting, same time, every 4 weeks. Changes are slow day to day but clear month to month.

When to talk to a doctor. Very skinny people who have tracked carefully, eaten well above estimated needs for several weeks, and still can't gain should get checked. Thyroid issues, digestive problems and other conditions can affect weight. Also talk to a doctor first if you have a medical condition or take medication that affects appetite or weight.
